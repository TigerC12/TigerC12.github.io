Robotics · Mechanical Summer 2026

Teleoperated robotic hand

Glove lead

A group project to build a robotic hand driven by an operator wearing a DOGlove, the open-source haptic force-feedback glove from the TEA Lab. I was responsible for the glove. The work was mostly mechanical: printing and fitting the 21-DoF joint linkage, assembling the cable-driven force-feedback transmission, and tuning the fit so the glove tracked cleanly through a full range of finger motion. On the electrical side I populated the board, flashed the firmware, and verified the glove was reporting finger positions to the hand. The rest of the team built the hand.

  • 21DoF hand tracking
  • 5fingers with force feedback
Mechanical 2025

3D filament recycler

A closed-loop machine that shreds failed PLA prints and extrudes them back into 1.75 mm filament. Over eight months a small team and I designed the shredder, extruder, and spooling stages, then iterated the nozzle geometry, spooling arm, and feed mechanism through five design revisions to hold diameter within ±0.05 mm, tight enough to print with. The finished machine went to our school's robotics team, which had been paying for filament out of a small budget.

PCB · Embedded 2025

Badminton racket accelerometer

A custom data-acquisition board that mounts to a badminton racket and records 3-axis swing acceleration and impact force at 1000 Hz. I designed it independently in KiCad: an ESP32-WROOM-32E reading an MPU-6050 IMU over I²C, USB-C with a CH340C bridge for programming and data offload, ESD protection on the data lines, and a TP4056 LiPo charger feeding a 3.3 V regulator so it runs untethered. The layout keeps the antenna clear with a keep-out zone and puts the battery footprint under the board to stay compact on the handle. I taught myself the circuit design and layout for this from component datasheets, designed a printed case to mount it to the racket, and used the data to analyze my own strokes.

  • 1000 Hzsample rate
  • 3-axisaccel + impact
